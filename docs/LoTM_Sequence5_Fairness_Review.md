# LoTM Sequence 5 Fairness Review

## Scope

This pass reviewed all current Sequence 5 abilities (`system.level = 4`) using the same internal power-budget lens as the earlier sequence reviews:

- overall combat and scene impact
- action economy efficiency
- spirituality cost versus payoff
- reliability and versatility
- scaling quality at higher spend
- synergy and abuse risk

The target remained relative parity, not role homogenization, while keeping each pathway aligned with its mechanical identity and the creation-guide expectation that Sequence 5 abilities should provide reliable encounter influence without erasing pathway weaknesses.

## Overall Balance Observations

- Sequence 5 is where many pathways first feel fully online. Most kits now have one stable encounter-defining tool, one specialist utility piece, and one signature escalation or transformation ability.
- Compared with Sequence 6, the average Sequence 5 package is a little swingier: there are more dramatic highs, but also more abilities whose baseline floor still depends on very specific terrain, setup, or GM framing.
- The main overperformance risk at this tier came from low-cost persistent control zones and zero-cost defensive states whose floor value was too easy to repeat.
- The main underperformance risk came from action-heavy abilities whose baseline output lagged too far behind comparable Sequence 5 control or damage packages.

## Identified Outliers

### Overperforming

- `Full Moon` (Apothecary): the baseline package delivered too much recurring zone control for only `1 Spirituality` without the normal concentration pressure seen on comparable Sequence 5 domains.
- `Unbreakable Defense` (Warrior): the baseline no-cost entry point gave too much repeatable defensive floor for a bonus-action stance that already imposed strong survivability and positioning denial.

### Underperforming

- `Precision Barrage` (Hunter): its baseline `+1 fire damage` rider was too low for a Sequence 5 action-and-spirituality spend, leaving the ability meaningfully behind neighboring AoE or commander-pressure tools until higher spend.
- `Positioning` (Apprentice): strong identity fit, but the baseline paid too little back in active combat for a `2 Spirituality` concentration buff centered on route logic.

### Near-Edge But Left Unchanged

- `Traveler's Door` is extremely strong mobility, but its high baseline spirituality cost, destination restrictions, and seal/anomaly failure cases keep it in band for Apprentice.
- `Guidance` is very efficient support, but its concentration, single-target scope, and anti-improvisation drawback preserve visible Spectator constraints.
- `Spirit Vessel` and `Child of the Oak` both sit near the high end of Sequence 5 floor value, but each remains appropriately bounded by concentration, mode limits, or pathway-role expectations.

## Applied Adjustments

### Full Moon

- Baseline duration now requires concentration.

Why:

- Brings the ability's recurring zone pressure back into line with other Sequence 5 battlefield domains.
- Preserves the moon-domain fantasy, terrain pressure, and anti-reaction identity.
- Keeps the stronger radius, debuff, and long-duration support riders as the real escalation points.

### Unbreakable Defense

- Baseline cost increased from `0 Spirituality` to `1 Spirituality`.

Why:

- Prevents Warrior from repeating an exceptionally strong defensive stance too freely at Sequence 5.
- Preserves the intended fortress-style identity rather than trimming away its defining resistance package.
- Keeps the duration, ally-cover, and nullification riders meaningful as higher-spend decisions instead of stacking on top of an overly cheap floor.

### Precision Barrage

- Baseline failed-save damage increased from `+1 fire damage` to damage equal to `Potency`.
- `+2 Spirituality` rider increased from `Potency` damage to `2 x Potency`.

Why:

- Gives Hunter's multi-target barrage a Sequence 5-appropriate damage floor.
- Preserves the identity of flexible formation pressure rather than turning the ability into single-target burst.
- Makes the baseline cast worth using before the higher-spend upgrade comes online.

### Positioning

- Baseline now also makes the first opportunity attack provoked against you during its indirect-path movement each turn occur with disadvantage.

Why:

- Adds a concrete skirmisher payoff to the route-logic fantasy.
- Improves active combat usefulness without changing the ability into teleportation or hard control.
- Keeps the ally-sharing and displacement-redirection riders as meaningful reasons to spend more.

## Remaining Concerns

- Sequence 5 is broadly healthy and needed a smaller touch pass than the lower sequences.
- Several zero-cost utility abilities remain very efficient, but they stay in band because their payoff is narrow, setup-bound, or strongly pathway-specific.
- Apprentice and Spectator still gain a lot of value from player creativity and GM interpretation at this tier. That is intended; this pass only raised the floor where it was clearly lagging behind.
