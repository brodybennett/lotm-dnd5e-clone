# LoTM Sequence 3 Fairness Review

## Scope

This pass reviewed all current Sequence 3 abilities (`system.level = 6`) using the same internal power-budget lens as the earlier sequence reviews:

- overall combat and scene impact
- action economy efficiency
- spirituality cost versus payoff
- reliability and versatility
- scaling quality at higher spend
- synergy and abuse risk

The goal remained relative parity rather than uniform role output, while preserving the expectation that Sequence 3 should feel like the point where saint-grade pathway authority becomes stable, broad, and encounter-defining.

## Overall Balance Observations

- Sequence 3 is where most pathways stop feeling like strong specialists and start feeling like true local authorities. Large domains, remote intervention, strong defensive reactions, and durable scene-control packages are common here.
- The tier is broadly healthy. Most pathways now have one authoritative battlefield or scene tool, one broad utility or infrastructure piece, and one high-ceiling expression of identity.
- The clearest overperformance risks were still zero-cost baseline effects, especially recurring zones that applied strong pressure every round without enough spending friction.
- The clearest underperformance risks were support packages whose fantasy was correct but whose baseline payoff lagged behind the rest of the tier's authority-level impact.

## Identified Outliers

### Overperforming

- `Plague Storm` (Spectator): the baseline `0 Spirituality` storm applied too much recurring AoE psychic pressure for a full-minute concentration zone at Sequence 3.
- `Nightmare World` (Sleepless): the baseline ally-selective zone-wide `1d4` offensive and utility penalty was too efficient at `0 Spirituality` for its size, range, and duration.

### Underperforming

- `Chain of Command` (Hunter): strong identity fit, but the baseline `+1 fire damage` rider was too low for a Sequence 3 commander-link that asks for an action, concentration, and allied coordination.
- `Prayer Echo` (Mystery Pryer): the remote reaction fantasy was excellent, but the baseline `+1` defensive nudge was too small for the tier unless the scene was already built around prayer-network play.

### Near-Edge But Left Unchanged

- `Hurricane of Light` is strong for a zero-cost baseline, but it is still a one-shot cone with backlash pressure rather than a persistent control engine.
- `Dream Weaving` is powerful, but its hostile-action damage is conditional and it remains bounded by single-target save reliance at baseline.
- `Hands of Life and Death` is efficient, but its short range and explicit split between offense and healing keep it within Corpse Collector's intended saint-grade lane.

## Applied Adjustments

### Plague Storm

- Baseline cost increased from `0 Spirituality` to `1 Spirituality`.

Why:

- Brings the recurring AoE damage floor back into line with other Sequence 3 zone authorities.
- Preserves the identity of a mental panic storm rather than trimming its core damage or disruption profile.
- Keeps the radius, mobility, and reaction-denial riders as meaningful escalation points.

### Nightmare World

- Baseline cost increased from `0 Spirituality` to `1 Spirituality`.

Why:

- Brings the zone's recurring penalty package closer to the intended Sequence 3 cost band.
- Preserves Sleepless' dream-horror domain identity and ally-selective concealment/tracking utility.
- Avoids a larger rework while still trimming the clearest floor-value excess.

### Chain of Command

- Baseline first-hit rider increased from `+1 fire damage` to fire damage equal to `Potency`.

Why:

- Gives Hunter's commander network a Sequence 3-appropriate baseline reward for coordinated play.
- Preserves the pathway's formation and tempo identity instead of turning the ability into generic passive buffing.
- Makes the action and concentration investment feel competitive before the higher-spend multi-hit and reaction-attack riders come online.

### Prayer Echo

- Baseline defensive bonus increased from `+1` to `+1d4` against the triggering attack or triggering save.

Why:

- Gives the reaction a more meaningful saint-tier defensive floor without changing its remote-omen identity.
- Keeps the movement rider and long-range symbolic protection intact.
- Leaves the stronger `+Potency`, cleanse, and rebound riders as real reasons to spend more.

## Remaining Concerns

- Sequence 3 is broadly healthy and needed another light-touch pass rather than a broad rebalance.
- Several zero-cost baselines remain intentionally strong because this tier is supposed to feel like stable authority play; this pass only trimmed the clearest recurring-pressure outliers.
- Some prayer, fate, and route abilities remain campaign-context sensitive by design. The goal here was to raise weak floors, not erase those pathway-specific dependencies.
