# LoTM Sequence 2 Fairness Review

## Scope

This pass reviewed all current Sequence 2 abilities (`system.level = 7`) using the same internal power-budget lens as the earlier sequence reviews:

- overall combat and scene impact
- action economy efficiency
- spirituality cost versus payoff
- reliability and versatility
- scaling quality at higher spend
- synergy and abuse risk

The goal remained relative parity rather than role uniformity, while preserving the expectation that Sequence 2 should feel like angel-transition authority: broad, scene-defining, and distinctly more oppressive than Sequence 3 without letting a handful of zero-cost effects dominate the whole tier.

## Overall Balance Observations

- Sequence 2 is the first tier where many pathways start expressing true angel-grade authority rather than just strong saint-level control. Large jurisdictions, revival hooks, broad conceptual edits, and high-impact support engines are common here.
- The tier is mostly healthy. Most pathways now present one major authority effect, one durable support or infrastructure piece, and one highly specialized high-ceiling expression of identity.
- The main overperformance risk remained zero-cost baseline powers whose floor value already resembled a full Sequence 2 encounter swing before any spending choice.
- The main underperformance risk came from support and prophecy tools whose thematic value was right, but whose baseline numbers no longer kept pace with the rest of the tier.

## Identified Outliers

### Overperforming

- `Nation of the Dead` (Corpse Collector): the baseline `0 Spirituality` underworld jurisdiction delivered too much broad recurring pressure and allied undead support for its size and duration.
- `Death Consul's Decree` (Corpse Collector): the baseline `0 Spirituality` execution strike compressed too much damage and kill pressure into a no-cost floor.

### Underperforming

- `Mentor Protocol` (Savant): strong support identity, but baseline payoff was too modest for Sequence 2 unless the table was heavily centered on repeated technical problem-solving.
- `Sage Counterfate` (Mystery Pryer): the baseline reaction was on-theme, but `+1 psychic damage` was too low to matter at this tier relative to the authority-level interruption it was trying to represent.

### Near-Edge But Left Unchanged

- `Dream Maze` is extremely strong, but it remains bounded by single-target save reliance and concentration.
- `Purifying Devastation` is efficient, but it is still a one-shot AoE burst rather than a persistent control engine.
- `Destination` and fate-route abilities like `Starfold Passage` and `Words of Fortune` are powerful, but their costs and scope are appropriate for Sequence 2 play.

## Applied Adjustments

### Nation of the Dead

- Baseline cost increased from `0 Spirituality` to `1 Spirituality`.

Why:

- Brings the recurring zone-control package closer to the intended Sequence 2 budget.
- Preserves Corpse Collector's broad underworld-jurisdiction identity instead of weakening its defining riders.
- Keeps the radius, silence, and soul-echo upgrades as meaningful escalation points.

### Death Consul's Decree

- Baseline cost increased from `0 Spirituality` to `1 Spirituality`.

Why:

- Prevents an execution-grade single-target strike from sitting at too cheap a floor for Sequence 2.
- Preserves the decree fantasy and lethal threshold mechanics rather than forcing a larger damage rewrite.
- Keeps the multi-target, strip-effect, and instant-death riders as meaningful higher-spend authority.

### Mentor Protocol

- Baseline now also lets each target reroll one failed protocol-related check once during the duration.

Why:

- Gives Savant a clearer immediate floor payoff in live scenes.
- Preserves the technical-training and distributed competence identity.
- Improves competitiveness without collapsing into generic universal buffing.

### Sage Counterfate

- Baseline damage increased from `+1 psychic damage` to psychic damage equal to `Potency`.
- `+2 Spirituality` damage increased from `Potency` to `2 x Potency`.

Why:

- Makes the prophecy interruption feel like a real Sequence 2 reaction instead of mostly a rider delivery tool.
- Preserves the future-fragment / counterfate identity rather than adding broader rewrite effects.
- Keeps the reaction-loss and temporary-effect suppression riders as higher-spend upgrades.

## Remaining Concerns

- Sequence 2 is broadly healthy and needed another narrow outlier pass rather than a broad rebalance.
- Zero-cost baselines remain common at this tier by design, but they need to be monitored carefully because the authority package size is much larger here than at lower sequences.
- Several support, analysis, and fate abilities are still campaign-context sensitive. The goal here was to improve weak floors, not erase the pathways' intended dependencies.
